The Science Behind HEPA Filtration

HEPA stands for High Efficiency Particulate Air, and the technology has been proven effective in hospitals, laboratories, and clean rooms for decades. A HEPA filter works by forcing air through a dense mat of fibers arranged randomly. Particles moving through the filter encounter these fibers and either get trapped or stick to them. The key to HEPA effectiveness is that it captures particles through three mechanisms: interception (particles follow air currents but get caught at the edges), impaction (larger particles hit the fibers directly), and diffusion (tiny particles bounce around and eventually get trapped). This multi-stage approach is why Mount Vernon's professional cleanup teams rely on HEPA systems rather than conventional home air filters.

HEPA stands for High-Efficiency Particulate Air. These filters capture 99. 97 percent of particles 0. 3 microns or larger. In the context of biohazard cleanup in Mount Vernon, HEPA filters remove bacteria, viruses, mold spores, and aerosolized biological materials from the air. When a trauma scene occurs, microorganisms become suspended in the air through aerosolization. These particles remain airborne for hours or days, depending on air currents and ventilation. A standard home air filter captures only 20 to 40 percent of these particles. HEPA filtration removes the vast majority. In Mount Vernon, homes require professional-grade HEPA systems to fully remediate contamination. These filters are essential to post-incident recovery.
