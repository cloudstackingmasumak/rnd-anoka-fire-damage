Practical guidance for Boulder property owners about checklist after a house fire, safety, professional cleanup, and the recovery process.

The hours and days after a house fire in Boulder are chaotic and overwhelming. You have just experienced a traumatic event, your home is damaged, and decisions need to happen fast. A clear checklist helps you stay on track and ensure nothing critical gets missed. This guide walks you through the essential actions that fire restoration professionals recommend for Boulder residents after a fire. Your first job is not to clean or decide what to salvage.

Document Everything Thoroughly

Ask your insurance company for a referral, or search for IICRC-certified companies in Boulder. Look for teams with strong local reviews, experience with fires similar to yours, and the ability to start work within 48 hours. A reputable company will come to your home, document damage, and provide a written scope of work before starting anything.

Document final completion with professional photos for your records and insurance file. Before moving back into your Boulder home, confirm that utilities are functioning safely, all systems have been tested and cleared, air quality is verified through testing, and the home has been thoroughly cleaned. Request a final walkthrough with family members to ensure you're ready for reoccupancy and to answer any questions about equipment or systems that were repaired or replaced.

Before anything else happens to your Boulder home, start documenting the damage with photographs and video. This is critical for your insurance claim. Take photos of the exterior of the house showing the damage. Take photos of each room showing what was damaged and what remains. Take photos of appliances, furniture, and personal belongings that were damaged. Take photos of water damage and wet areas where firefighting water spread. Take photos of intact areas too, showing what was not damaged. Use your smartphone or a camera to record video walking through each room so you have a continuous record of the damage.

The First Day: Safety and Documentation

The immediate hours after fire discovery demand focused action and clear thinking despite emotional stress. Contact your fire department immediately if fires are active or if you haven't already evacuated. Verify that every family member has reached a safe location away from smoke and flames. Account for pets and make a quick phone list of people to contact about evacuation and temporary housing.

Expect restoration work to progress through stages including removal of charred materials, deep cleaning of smoke and soot damage, drying of water-damaged structures, and replacement of damaged systems. Not all work happens simultaneously, as some phases must complete before others can begin. Air quality improvement through ventilation and filtration happens throughout the restoration process. Odor remediation may take weeks or months as layers of cleaning remove residual smoke compounds. Boulder homeowners benefit from understanding these phases so they can plan for temporary housing and manage expectations about restoration timelines.

Immediate Safety and Access

Contact family members to let them know you are safe after the fire at your Boulder home. Only when fire officials confirm the building is safe should anyone enter the property.

Your safety comes before anything else. Once firefighters clear your Boulder home, do not enter if any official has told you to stay out. Even if the fire itself is out, structural damage, carbon monoxide, or gas leaks can still pose real danger. If you are safe to enter briefly, wear a mask or respirator because soot and ash are not just dirty, they are harmful to breathe in large amounts.

Contact a fire damage restoration company early in the process. Do not wait for your insurance adjuster's approval to hire restoration professionals. The sooner restoration work begins, the better the outcome. Restoration professionals can perform the initial assessment and recommend what work should happen first. They can help you understand the scope of restoration needed before the adjuster arrives.

Plan Your Recovery Timeline

If your property sits near other homes in Boulder, notify your neighbors that the fire was contained to your home and no ongoing risk exists. This reduces their anxiety and prevents unnecessary calls to fire authorities. If the fire spread to adjacent properties, begin separate coordination with those neighbors and their insurance companies.

Call your insurance company. Have your policy number ready. Report the fire and give them the basic facts: time, location, cause if you know it, and a rough estimate of damage. The insurance company will assign you an adjuster. Get the adjuster's name and phone number. This person will become your main point of contact for the claim.

Contact your homeowner insurance company as soon as you are safe. In Boulder, do not wait. Insurance companies have 24-hour claims lines. Report the fire, provide your policy number, and give them your location and contact information. Do not minimize or exaggerate damage. Stick to facts.

Your insurance adjuster will be assigned within hours or days. Adjusters inspect fire damage and determine what is covered. They coordinate with restoration contractors and approve payment for repairs. Having clear documentation and being responsive to your adjuster speeds up the claim process.

Arrange Housing and Prevent Secondary Loss

A dehumidifier or two can help reduce moisture, but professional water extraction is much more effective. This should be your priority immediately after the fire, not weeks later. The first forty-eight hours are critical for preventing mold and additional water damage.

Check on neighbors in Boulder who may have been affected by smoke drift or adjacent fire spread. If you smell smoke in nearby homes, report it to the fire department so they can do a quick inspection. Many fires in residential neighborhoods create hazards beyond the primary burn site. Take care of your community, and they will take care of you.

After moving back into your home, monitor for lingering issues like odors that emerge on warm days, new water leaks developing in previously treated areas, or mold growth in spaces that were previously treated. Report these issues immediately to the restoration company rather than hoping they resolve on their own. Many post-fire problems emerge weeks or months after initial restoration and may be covered under restoration warranties or insurance policies.

Keep all receipts, completion certificates, warranty documentation, and professional assessments organized in a secure location for future reference. Maintain these records for at least five years in case questions arise about the fire damage or restoration work completed. This documentation protects you if future issues develop and supports potential claims related to the restoration work.

Get Professional Assessment

After professionals arrive, they will photograph damage, measure affected areas, and develop a detailed assessment. This assessment determines what can be cleaned, what must be replaced, and the overall restoration timeline. Professionals test for structural damage, verify HVAC system integrity, and check for asbestos or other hazardous materials. The assessment includes checking insulation in walls and attic spaces for contamination. Professional assessment identifies problems that homeowners miss, such as damage to joists, rafters, or framing. The assessment results in a detailed scope of work that your insurance company reviews and approves. This scope document becomes the contract for restoration work and the basis for insurance payment. The assessment phase typically takes one to three days depending on damage extent. Once the scope is approved, work can begin immediately.

Discuss temporary measures your contractor will put in place, like dehumidifiers and air movers to prevent mold, and how long structural drying will take. Most Boulder homes require weeks of mechanical drying to prevent hidden mold behind walls and in attics. Ask about daily monitoring and progress updates.

Never assume it is safe to go back inside the damaged home until a firefighter explicitly tells you so and confirms the structure is sound and safe for entry.

Start a separate list of items you might be able to salvage and restore through professional cleaning. Furniture, textiles, electronics, documents, and personal items sometimes recover with professional care. Separate these from items that are clearly a total loss. Your restoration company in Boulder can advise which items are worth attempting to restore.
