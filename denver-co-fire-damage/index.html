In Denver, where hurricane season and summer thunderstorms pose constant threats, a damaged roof is a disaster waiting to become worse. Tarps are extended beyond the roof edge and secured with rope and anchors to withstand gusts and heavy rain. Each tarp is inspected regularly and reinforced as conditions demand because Colorado weather can quickly compromise even well-installed temporary coverings. For extensive roof damage, temporary sheathing or structural plywood may provide better protection than tarps alone, offering more durability and reliability. The goal is to hold back rain and wind until permanent roofing repair can be completed.

In some cases, temporary support structures are installed to support critical areas while repairs are made. These temporary supports prevent structural failure while work is ongoing. Once repairs are complete, temporary supports are removed. This phased approach allows safe restoration work without putting anyone at risk.

What Board-Up Services Protect Against in Denver

Beyond human threats, fire-damaged homes attract wildlife seeking shelter. Raccoons, squirrels, birds, and other animals enter through openings and take residence in the structure. Once established, removing animals is difficult and expensive. Animals may cause additional damage by chewing wiring, insulation, and wood. They create biohazards through feces and urine accumulation.

Proper boarding prevents animal entry by sealing all openings completely. No gaps should be large enough for small animals to pass through. Vents and other necessary openings must be screened or otherwise protected. Once animals have entered, removal services are required. Preventing entry through complete boarding is far more efficient than removing established animal populations later.

Beyond physical board-up, utilities must be made safe. An active gas line in a fire-damaged home is an explosion hazard. Professionals coordinate with gas utility companies to shut off gas at the meter. The main valve is locked or sealed so it cannot be accidentally reactivated. In Denver, gas shutoff is a non-negotiable safety step after fire. Electrical systems in a fire-damaged Denver home are similarly hazardous. Professionals work with licensed electricians to isolate electrical hazards. The main panel might be shut down entirely, or specific circuits are isolated if the system is partially salvageable. Professionals do not want anyone receiving electrical shock from your Denver home after fire. Water systems might need shutoff too, depending on damage.
