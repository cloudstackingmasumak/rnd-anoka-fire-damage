Mistake Eight: Not Coordinating with Your Restoration Professional

Some homeowners make decisions about restoration without consulting their restoration professional. They authorize certain work or approve materials without professional input. They think they are saving money or making the process faster. Instead, they create inefficiencies and problems.

Your restoration professional understands the best sequence for work. They know which materials will serve you best. They coordinate with your insurance company based on claim coverage. When you make decisions outside their input, you risk authorizing work that insurance will not cover. You might choose materials that will not perform well in your situation. You might approve work that needs to be redone. Trust your restoration professional's guidance. They have done this hundreds of times in Clearwater.

Professional assessment includes water damage mapping using moisture meters to identify saturation in walls, flooring, structural members, and insulation. Specialized drying equipment like commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers must run continuously for days or weeks depending on saturation depth and material types. Professional crews manages this systematically, testing moisture levels repeatedly until wood content and structural materials reach safe, dry conditions that prevent mold and decay.

Skipping Hidden Moisture Checks

Moisture hidden inside walls and crawl spaces creates conditions perfect for mold, wood rot, and insect infestation. Detecting this hidden moisture requires specialized equipment including moisture meters and thermal imaging. Homeowners cannot reliably identify wet insulation or damp framing inside walls just by looking or touching accessible surfaces. Professional moisture mapping discovers these problem areas.

Crawl spaces under homes are particularly prone to hidden moisture problems after fires. Water from firefighting drains into crawl space areas and settles around foundation walls and support beams. Wood-destroying insects thrive in wet crawl spaces. Wood rot weakens structural beams. Professional teams remove standing water from crawl spaces and use dehumidifiers to dry these enclosed spaces completely.
